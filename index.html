Gate capacitance: C–V curve

Low frequency High frequency VFB Vth Present VG

At low frequency the inversion carriers follow the AC signal, so C climbs back to Cox in strong inversion. At high frequency they cannot, so C stays at the minimum set by the maximum depletion width. In a MOSFET, the source and drain supply inversion carriers, so the measured gate capacitance follows the low-frequency curve; the high-frequency curve is what an isolated MOS capacitor shows.

Model assumptions

Long-channel square-law model with constant mobility, smoothly extended into subthreshold through Veff. The body is held at 0 V and the body effect is ignored. Vth is calculated from the gate work function, substrate doping and tox, with no oxide charge and no poly-gate depletion. n⁺ and p⁺ poly gates are treated as degenerate, with φm = χ and χ + Eg/q.

Below about L = 0.25 µm, real devices depart from the square law because of velocity saturation and other short-channel effects, so treat curves at the low end of the L range as idealized.

The silicon is 3L long and 0.5 µm tall. Source and drain are each L × W × 0.2 µm. The gate is L × W × 0.2 µm. Switching between NMOS and PMOS mirrors every voltage and swaps n⁺ and p⁺ poly, so the PMOS is the exact complement of the NMOS.
